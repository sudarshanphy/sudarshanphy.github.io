I am Sudarshan, a fifth-year physics Ph.D. candidate in the [Department of Physics and Astronomy](https://physics.utk.edu/) at the [University of Tennessee Knoxville](https://www.utk.edu/). I study how massive stars end their lives in spectacular [explosions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernova), in particular [core-collapse supernova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_II_supernova#Core_collapse) explosions. My research uses large-scale computer simulations to explore how these explosions unfold over time and how they shape the elements and structures we observe in the universe today. This work is being carried out under the supervision of [Dr. William R. Hix](https://www.ornl.gov/staff-profile/w-raphael-hix) and [Dr. Bronson Messer](https://www.ornl.gov/staff-profile/bronson-e-messer-ii).

Before joining Ph.D., I completed my M.S. in Physics at the [University of Massachusetts Dartmouth](https://www.umassd.edu/engineering/phy/) under the supervision of [Dr. Robert Fisher](https://sites.google.com/site/fishercompgroup). There, I studied [thermonuclear (Type Ia) supernovae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_Ia_supernova), the powerful explosions of white dwarfs that serve as crucial tools for understanding cosmic distances and chemical enrichment.
